# Citing Elómë

If Elómë contributes to your research, please cite the software paper:

> Kipping, D. (in preparation). *Elómë: analytic, differentiable transit
> light curves on Apple Silicon.* Journal of Open Source Software.

The reference, DOI and BibTeX entry will appear here once the paper is
published. Until then, please cite the software by its repository,
<https://github.com/davidkipping/elome>, and the version you used.

## The hybrid limb-darkening laws

If you use the hybrid laws (`hybrid2`, `hybrid4`, `hybrid5`), or model an
oblate or ringed planet or a spotted star, please also cite:

- **The hybrid-law paper**, which introduces the laws and proves that they
  give elementary transit light curves for spherical and oblate planets
  (in preparation).
- **The hybrid-law coefficients paper**, which tunes the laws' poles and
  tabulates their limb-darkening coefficients for stellar models and
  passbands (in preparation).

## The methods Elómë builds on

- The spherical light curve: Agol, Luger & Foreman-Mackey (2020),
  *AJ* 159, 123, [arXiv:1908.03222](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03222).
- The limb-darkening priors for the quadratic law: Kipping (2013),
  *MNRAS* 435, 2152, [arXiv:1308.0009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0009).
- The eccentric Kepler solver: Markley (1995), *Celestial Mechanics and
  Dynamical Astronomy* 63, 101.
- The frontend's design follows batman: Kreidberg (2015), *PASP* 127,
  1161, [arXiv:1507.08285](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08285).
- Starspots as circular caps on the stellar surface: Kipping (2012),
  *MNRAS* 427, 2487, [arXiv:1209.2985](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2985).
- The sky-projected obliquity convention: Fabrycky & Winn (2009), *ApJ*
  696, 1230, [arXiv:0902.0737](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.0737).
